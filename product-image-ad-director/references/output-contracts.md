# Output Contracts

Use these structures to keep the skill consistent.

## Stage 1: Concept Stage Output

When the user has provided only a product image or vague intent, return:

### A. Product Read

Keep it short:

- what the product most likely is
- what the visible package says about tone
- what assumptions you are making

### B. Market Read

Keep it short:

- 2 to 4 category truths
- 2 to 4 cliches to avoid
- 1 sentence on what feels fresher

### C. Direction Board

Give 3 to 5 directions. For each direction include:

- `title`
- `style`
- `hook`
- `how the viewer first misreads the video`
- `product role`
- `reveal method`
- `why it fits`
- `main risk`

### D. Recommendation Layer

Explicitly tag:

- best for spread
- best for selling
- best for VFX
- least ad-like
- your recommended first direction

### E. Confirmation Step

Tell the user to confirm, merge, or revise directions.
The user may keep one direction, multiple directions, or a merged hybrid.

Do not move to fully detailed scripts until the user confirms unless they already requested direct scripting.

## Stage 2: Detailed Script Output

Once the user confirms one or more directions, create a full script block for each.

Each script block must include:

### 1. Core Premise

- one-sentence summary

### 2. Strategic Goal

- what this version optimizes for
- spread, sell-through, premium brand feel, VFX spectacle, or story disguise

### 3. Viewer Hook

- what the viewer thinks they are watching in the opening

### 4. Product Integration Logic

- why the product appears naturally
- why the reveal does not feel forced

### 5. Scene Logic

- setting
- character role
- conflict or tension
- resolution logic

### 6. Beat Map

Default to a 15-second beat map unless otherwise requested:

- `0-3s`
- `3-6s`
- `6-10s`
- `10-13s`
- `13-15s`

### 7. Shot Plan

For each beat define:

- shot size
- framing
- camera move
- visual task

### 8. Light and Color

- dominant palette
- key light character
- contrast level
- what must stay visually consistent

### 9. VFX and Practical Effects

- what effect appears
- what triggers it
- what it means in story terms
- what to avoid

### 10. Sound Design

- environment bed
- foley
- impact layer
- music direction
- silence or drop-out moments

### 11. Performance Logic

- how the character should behave
- what emotion should be visible
- what must stay restrained

### 12. Do-Not-Miss Notes

- 3 to 6 production-critical notes

### 13. Optional Seedance Notes

When useful, add:

- best number of shots
- subject count caution
- camera-move caution
- continuity anchors

## Refinement Contract

If the user asks to revise after script stage, revise by dimension rather than rebuilding blindly:

- stronger opening
- smoother reveal
- more premium
- more funny
- more VFX
- less ad-like
- better Seedance compatibility
