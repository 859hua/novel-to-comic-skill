# Style Taxonomy

Use this file to decide which ad styles fit the product naturally.

## Selection Rule

Do not ask "what looks coolest."

Ask:

- what visual temperament the product already has
- what dramatic ability its promise can map to
- what style will feel natural instead of pasted on
- whether the goal is selling, spreading, worldbuilding, or spectacle

## Primary Style Families

### 1. Direct-response functional

Use for:

- clear problem-solution products
- quick sell-through
- products that need clarity over mood

Works well for:

- cleaning
- basic skincare
- home tools
- household products

Risk:

- looks like obvious ad immediately

### 2. Premium cinematic

Use for:

- high-end packaging
- prestige skincare
- fragrance
- design-led products

Works well for:

- glass, metal, sculptural, or minimalist packaging

Risk:

- becomes empty luxury wallpaper if the concept has no dramatic spine

### 3. Story disguise

Use for:

- short-video environments where viewers skip obvious ads
- products that can hide inside a scene or role

Works well for:

- beauty
- skincare
- wearable tech
- everyday products with a strong ritual or role-based use

Risk:

- if reveal comes too early, the disguise collapses

### 4. System / scan / cyber

Use for:

- white, metallic, clinical, or device-like products
- products whose promise can map to reset, control, clarity, lock-on, cleanup

Works well for:

- cleansers
- repair items
- tech devices
- men's functional products

Risk:

- cheap UI overlays and random "futuristic" language

### 5. Fantasy / mythic / purification

Use for:

- products that feel like cleansing, shielding, restoring, reviving, dissolving, or revealing

Works well for:

- cleansing
- repair
- fragrance
- protective products

Risk:

- can drift too far from product truth if the metaphor gets uncontrolled

### 6. High-concept VFX spectacle

Use for:

- products with strong visual contrast
- campaigns where attention and wow-factor matter

Works well for:

- clean white products
- bold black products
- transformation products
- products with a strong before/after tension

Risk:

- spectacle with no product logic

### 7. Documentary or pseudo-program

Use for:

- content-native social formats
- clever commentary
- products that benefit from commentary, reporting, scanning, diagnosis, or tutorials

Examples:

- weather report
- late-night bulletin
- pseudo-diagnosis
- micro-documentary

Risk:

- can become gimmicky if there is no product-specific insight

### 8. Emotional ritual

Use for:

- products tied to ending the day, resetting, calming down, dressing up, going out, recovering, or self-return

Works well for:

- skincare
- fragrance
- wellness
- makeup removal

Risk:

- emotionally nice but weak on product distinctiveness

### 9. Action-integrated

Use for:

- products whose ability can map to clearing, breaking, shielding, wiping, recovering, or restoring control

Works well for:

- cleansing
- men's products
- tech devices
- tools
- high-energy functional products

Risk:

- if the product is "taken out" mid-fight, it feels like an ad. Embed it in the world logic instead.

### 10. Comic absurdist

Use for:

- social spread
- playful brands
- youth-facing content

Works well for:

- snacks
- novelty products
- fun beauty
- casual household items

Risk:

- hurts premium positioning if used on the wrong product

## Quick Match Heuristics

### White, clean, minimal tube or bottle

Bias toward:

- premium cinematic
- system / scan / cyber
- fantasy purification
- emotional ritual

### Dark, metallic, angular packaging

Bias toward:

- system / scan / cyber
- action-integrated
- high-concept VFX spectacle

### Cute, colorful, rounded packaging

Bias toward:

- comic absurdist
- story disguise
- documentary or pseudo-program

### Clinical or medical cues

Bias toward:

- system / scan / cyber
- direct-response functional

Be careful with:

- fantasy that overclaims
- silly comedy that harms trust

## Output Rule

In concept stage, do not propose every possible style.

Instead:

- recommend 1 primary path
- include 2 to 4 strong alternates
- explicitly state which styles you are rejecting and why
